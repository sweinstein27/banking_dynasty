# Ventures and railways, 1815-1850

Purpose: realistic durations, cost overruns and returns for the game's "ventures" (railway, shipping, canal, mill). Figures are as stated by the cited page; items marked **Uncertain** are listed at the end.

## German projects

| Project | Start of work | Opening | Build time | Cost vs plan | Returns |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nuremberg-Fuerth (Ludwigseisenbahn), 6.03 km | 1835 | 7 Dec 1835 | under 1 year | planned 132,000 fl, cost about 170,000 fl (+29%) | 20% paid in 1836; 12% or more until 1855 |
| Leipzig-Dresden, 116 km | ground broken 1 Mar 1836 (company founded 22 May 1835) | first section 24 Apr 1837; full 7 Apr 1839; two-track 1 Oct 1840 | about 3 years to opening, 4.5 to full double track | initial engineers' estimate 1.81 to 1.96 million thaler; capital 4.5 million (1837), later raised to 6.5 million | dividends not found (**Uncertain**) |

| Main-Neckar (Frankfurt-Heidelberg) | June 1843 (Frankfurt section) | Darmstadt-Langen trial 16 Apr 1846; Frankfurt-Heidelberg through service Aug 1846 | about 3 years | cost not found; Baden bore one sixth of construction cost for one sixth of net income | not found |
| Ludwig Canal (Main-Danube), 172.4 km, about 100 locks | 1 Jul 1836 | Nuremberg-Bamberg 6 May 1843; handed to company 2 Jul 1846 | about 10 years against 6 planned | planned 8 million fl, actual 17.5 million fl (+119%); share capital 10 million fl | 4% interest guaranteed from 1 Jul 1842 by Rothschild contract if not finished; 1852 income 160,671 fl vs costs 94,145 fl; profits about 50,000 fl a year 1850-63, then losses (2.9 million fl by 1912) |

Ludwig Canal traffic: 100,000 t a year planned; about 175,000 t in 1847; peak just under 196,000 t in 1850. The railway Nuremberg-Bamberg was built almost at the same time and partly parallel; no through shipping Rhine-Danube was possible because river boats were too wide for the canal and canal boats too deep-draught for Main and Danube (same source). The English page says the canal's narrow channel, many locks and limited water supply made it uneconomic ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Canal)).

Taunus note: an old guidebook (OCR unclear) is reported to give "3 1/2 million gulden" and "two years" of construction, and shares of 250 fl. each (search result, not opened; **Uncertain**).

## Rhine steam

- Prussian-Rhenish Steamship Company (PRDG), Cologne: founded 3 Oct 1825 by Cologne merchants (English Wikipedia, [Dampfschiffahrts-Gesellschaft fuer den Nieder- und Mittelrhein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dampfschiffahrts-Gesellschaft_f%C3%BCr_den_Nieder-_und_Mittelrhein)). A search summary also cites 1826 registration dates and a Cologne-Mainz service from 1827 with the *Concordia* (**Uncertain**, not verified on a page I opened).
- Rival Mainz company (Rhein und Main) founded about the same time. Riparian governments blocked competition, and the companies shared the market with the Dutch NSM; once shipping became freer, the British General Steam Navigation Company wanted in with substantial capital (same page).
- Duesseldorf company (Niederrhein, "DGNM"): approved 22 Sep 1836; capital 550,000 thaler in 2,750 shares of 200 thaler (same page). In 1868 gross revenue 303,000 thaler, net profit 71,000 thaler (a 13% net-to-capital figure, my arithmetic, 71/550).
- The two companies cooperated from 1853 as "Koeln-Duesseldorfer" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ln-D%C3%BCsseldorfer), via search summary).

## British comparisons

- Stockton-Darlington: Act 19 Apr 1821, track laying from 23 May 1822, opened 27 Sep 1825 (about 4 years from Act); costs "greatly exceeded estimates"; 5% promised, 8% by 1832; no dividend 1848 and following years; 4% in 1852 ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_and_Darlington_Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_and_Darlington_Railway)).
- Liverpool-Manchester: company 1824, Act 5 May 1826, opened 15 Sep 1830 (4 years from Act); average dividend 9.5% over 15 years independent; cost quoted as 637,000 pounds, tagged "citation needed" ([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_and_Manchester_Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_and_Manchester_Railway); [Railway Mania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_Mania)). Stephenson's early 40,000 pound quote versus Giles at 200,000 shows the range of estimates.
- Railway Mania: 263 Acts in 1846; schemes of 9,500 miles; 6,220 miles built from 1844-46 authorizations; about one third of authorized lines never built ([Railway Mania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_Mania)). Share index fell from 167.9 (summer 1845) to 60.5 (end 1849), -64%; track in service 2,240 miles (1844) to 6,621 (1850); revenue per mile fell from 3,280 to 2,080 pounds (Odlyzko, via search summary of [www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/%7Eodlyzko/doc/mania06.pdf)). Prices rose about 102% 1843 to Aug 1845 (Campbell, same search).
- Textile mills: no usable source found (**Uncertain**).

## For the game

Suggested venture archetypes (values are design choices anchored on the table):

| Type | Length (turns = quarters) | Cost overrun | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short line or workshop (Nuremberg-Fuerth) | 4 to 8 | +10 to +30% | high yield possible, 10 to 20% |
| Regional railway (Taunus, Leipzig-Dresden, Main-Neckar) | 12 to 20 | +0 to +50% (**Uncertain**) | slow ramp, 4 to 12% |
| Canal or very large works (Ludwig) | 24 to 40 | +100% | 0 to 4%, tail risk of lasting losses |
| Steamship line | 8 to 16 | n/a | monopoly rents, then competition |
| Mania scheme (1844-47) | 12+ | high; one third never built | -60% on shares after peak |

Put overrun and returns in `data/tuning.json`; trigger a "mania" event 1844-46 and a bust 1846-49.
