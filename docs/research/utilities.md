# Gasworks and waterworks, 1815-1850

Research note for the game. Only facts seen in fetched pages or search results are used. Gaps are flagged **Uncertain**.

## Gas lighting

| Place | Date | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

| Hamburg | 1845 | William Lindley designed a gas plant for street and house lighting; Grace's Guide says the works was on the "Grasbrook" | [Wikipedia, William Lindley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lindley), [Grace's Guide](https://gracesguide.co.uk/William_Lindley) (search summaries) |

Correction to the brief: the 1828 Frankfurt works is credited to Knoblauch & Schiele, not to ICGA. ICGA's Frankfurt link is the 1844 Obermainstrasse works. A gas-industry chronology scan lists Knoblauch and Schiele with 1828 but the scan was garbled, so the date-to-city alignment there is **Uncertain**; the Wikipedia statement is the main support.

How financed: ICGA was a London joint-stock company operating under Parliamentary Acts (1833, 1847 and later); the pages give no capital figures. Berlin's gas was a foreign concession on a fixed 21-year term. Frankfurt's 1828 works was a private-firm operation. **No capital or share-price data was found for any of these.**

## Waterworks

- Frankfurt: Wikipedia states the city's water supply was built 1827 to 1834 ([Free City of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Frankfurt)). No detail on technology or financing was found. **Uncertain.**
- Frankfurt: planning of a 70 km spring-water pipeline from the Vogelsberg and Spessart began 1869, commissioned 1873, driven by population growth ([Historisches Museum Frankfurt](https://www.historisches-museum-frankfurt.de/iart/os-download-object/91239)). Outside our period.
- William Lindley (English engineer) advanced Frankfurt's drainage and groundwater pumping works and the Vogelsberg and Spessart plans ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz53546.html)); the dates of his Frankfurt work were not seen. **Uncertain.**
- Hamburg: Lindley reported on the water supply in February 1844 after the 1842 fire; one source says the Stadtwasserkunst opened in 1848 ([Wikipedia, William Lindley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lindley), search summary). **Uncertain** on the 1848 date.
- Berlin: contract 14 Dec 1852 between the police president and English contractors Fox and Crampton, 25 years from 1 Jul 1856; first waterworks ran 1 Apr 1856 (Landesarchiv Berlin, via search summary of the sources above). Outside our period, but shows the pattern of English concessions.

## For the game

- Event chain for a Frankfurt-style city: "Gas lighting proposal" around 1828 (private firm sells shares), "foreign gas concession" around 1844 (a London company), "waterworks scheme" late in the period.
- Pattern from the sources: gas came first through private firms and foreign companies, municipal water later. Use these as unlock windows: gas 1826 to 1845 across cities, water 1844 onward.
- Concession mechanics: fixed term (Berlin 21 years for gas, 25 years for water) is real data and can set contract length.
- Financing numbers (capital, dividends) are not sourced; set invented values in `data/tuning.json` and mark them as tuned.
- Real firm names (Knoblauch & Schiele, ICGA) are fine as historical flavour; the player's own ventures should be invented.
