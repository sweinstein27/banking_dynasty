# Historical research

Nine dated, sourced notes for the later milestones: bourse-1815-1850, utilities, commodities-and-harvests, ventures-and-railways, sovereign-loans, currencies, politics-and-war, technology and interest-rates. Each ends with "For the game" and "Uncertain" sections.

**Most important uncertain points.** Dates are mostly solid (Zollverein 1834, Frankfurt joined 1836; Nuremberg-Fuerth Dec 1835; Taunus railway 1839-40; Prussian loan of 1818; Bank of England Consol yields from FRED). Hard money numbers are thin: no verified dividends or share prices for the Taunus or Main-Neckar railways, no Frankfurt prices for the 1825-26 and 1847-48 crashes, no loan commission percentages, no Frankfurt or Hamburg discount rates, no German usury caps, and no Frankfurt grain, wine or fair figures. The Frankfurt gasworks of 1828 is credited to Knoblauch and Schiele on one source (not Imperial Continental, whose Frankfurt works date from 1844), and the 1827-34 waterworks rests on one line. Greek loan sizes and default year (1826 or 1827), the Paulskirche end date (30 or 31 May 1849) and the sterling gold standard date are disputed or unchecked. Several facts come from search summaries or OCR text, and are flagged in the files. Commission ranges, overrun ranges and FX shock sizes are design assumptions, not facts. For the game: a 5% base rate fits 1815-22 but about 4% fits 1825-50; 6% good-borrower caps are plausible; the 10% and 16% caps are unverifiable design choices; 5-6% coupons suit 1818 but 3-4% suits strong credit later. The "Rhine shipping" venture before 1826 needs a sail or barge name, and Naples should become the Two Sicilies from December 1816.
