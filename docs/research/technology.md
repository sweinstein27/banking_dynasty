# Technology in the German lands, 1815-1850

Only facts seen in the cited pages are stated. Gaps are marked **Uncertain** and listed at the end.

## Steam on the water

- 1816: the British steamer Defiance reached Cologne and turned back; a year later the Caledonia reached Koblenz. Defiance returned to England for repairs and did not come back (search summary of https://the-duesseldorfer.de/schon-1816-die-defiance-das-erste-dampfschiff-auf-dem-rhein/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dampfschiffahrts-Gesellschaft_f%C3%BCr_den_Nieder-_und_Mittelrhein).
- 3 October 1825: the Preussisch-Rheinische Dampfschiffahrts-Gesellschaft (PRDG) was founded in Cologne by merchants led by Peter Heinrich Merkens; a Mainz company (DGRM) is also dated 1825 (same sources, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ln-D%C3%BCsseldorfer).
- 1 May 1827: Concordia made her first Mainz-Cologne trip; the PRDG ran Cologne-Mainz and the DGRM the upper stretch (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_(1826)). Sources disagree on which boat was "first".
- Steam engines in industry (mining, textiles, machine works): not sourced. **Uncertain.**

## Telegraph

- 1830: Berlin-Potsdam optical line (three stations); 1837 Hamburg-Cuxhaven commercial optical line (closed 1850); 1847 Bremen-Bremerhaven (closed 1852) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_telegraph).
- 1833: Prussian optical (semaphore) line Berlin-Magdeburg-Dortmund-Cologne-Bonn-Koblenz completed, about 200 staff, decommissioned 1849 for an electric line (same page). The "1832 order" date was not confirmed. **Uncertain.**
- 1833: Gauss and Weber, Goettingen, 1,200 m wire; 1837 Cooke and Wheatstone patent (May); 1838 Steinheil's earth-return line along Nuremberg-Fuerth railway; 24 May 1844 Morse, Washington-Baltimore (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_telegraph).
- 1848-49: Siemens and Halske built the Berlin-Frankfurt government line to reach the National Assembly; opened early 1849 (February per one source); length quoted from 500 to 670 km, so the figure is **Uncertain** (search results incl. https://ethw.org/Werner_von_Siemens and https://www.vde.com/de/geschichte/chronik/telegrafie, snippets only).

## Gas lighting

Frankfurt's first gasworks, Knoblauch & Schiele, began supplying private households in 1828 (English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Frankfurt, via search summary). Hannover had street gas lighting in 1825 and Berlin's first gasworks in 1826 (German search result, https://germanhistorydocs.org/de/vom-vormaerz-bis-zur-preussischen-vorherrschaft-1815-1866/verlegung-der-roehren-fuer-die-erste-gasanstalt-berlins-1826). Month and public-street start for Frankfurt: **Uncertain.**

## Zollverein and industry

- 26 May 1818: Prussian internal customs union; Zollverein treaties 1833, effective 1 January 1834; Frankfurt joined 2 January 1836; Baden, Nassau, Hesse-Homburg 1835; Hanover not until 1851 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Customs_Union). Its effect on Prussian industrialization is disputed by revisionists (same page).
- Other Frankfurt context: Talbot photograph of Hauptwache 1846 (German Wikipedia history page).
