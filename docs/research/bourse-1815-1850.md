# Frankfurt bourse, 1815-1850

Research note for the game. Sources were fetched in this session; where a source was silent the gap is stated and flagged **Uncertain**. Unit note: the Frankfurt gulden (guilder) was divided into 60 kreuzer ([Wikipedia, Free City of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Frankfurt)).

## What traded and when

| Date | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1820 | First share listed in Frankfurt: shares of the Austrian National Bank traded for the first time | [Deutsche Boerse, history of the Frankfurt exchange](https://live.deutsche-boerse.com/en/know-how/about/geschichte-der-frankfurter-wertpapierboerse/18th-and-19th-centuries-citizens-princes-new-stock-exchanges) |
| 1820-1830 | Rothschild displaced the Bethmann bank as leading issuer of government bonds in the German-speaking region | [Wikipedia, M. A. Rothschild & Soehne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._A._Rothschild_%26_S%C3%B6hne) |
| 1835 | Bethmann and Rothschild head the consortium for the Taunus railway; Rothschild normally avoided industrial shares, so this was an exception | same Wikipedia page; [Wikipedia, Taunus Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunus_Railway) |
| 12 Aug 1838 | Taunus-Eisenbahn-Gesellschaft founded in Frankfurt; shares "immediately oversubscribed 40 times" | [Wikipedia, Taunus Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunus_Railway) |
| 26 Sep 1839 | Taunus line opens Frankfurt to Hoechst; Hattersheim 24 Nov 1839; Mainz-Kastel 13 Apr 1840; Wiesbaden 19 May 1840 | same |
| 1843 | First own exchange building (Alte Boerse); assembly moved in on 11 Dec | Deutsche Boerse page above; [German Wikipedia, Frankfurter Wertpapierboerse](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Wertpapierbörse) |
| 1 Aug 1846 | Main-Neckar line (Frankfurt/Darmstadt-Heidelberg) opens for passengers; freight from 9 Aug 1847. Founding treaty between Frankfurt, Hesse and Baden dated 25 Feb 1843 | [German Wikipedia, Main-Neckar-Eisenbahn-Gesellschaft](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-Neckar-Eisenbahn-Gesellschaft) |
| by 1850 | The exchange was mainly a market for safe government bonds and funds ("solid Frankfurt"); shares were a smaller, later and more limited market than on other big exchanges | Deutsche Boerse page above |

Frankfurt was also a "gateway to capital exports": foreign bonds were placed from there on other European exchanges (Deutsche Boerse page).

Main-Neckar background: its predecessor, the Darmstaedter Eisenbahn-Gesellschaft (founded 31 Jan 1836, dissolved end of 1841), needed 6 million gulden of share capital and failed to raise it ([German Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-Neckar-Eisenbahn-Gesellschaft)). The 1843 treaty split the initial rolling stock cost 2/3 Hesse, 1/6 each for Frankfurt and Baden (same page). So the Main-Neckar line was effectively state-backed, not a pure private share issue.

## 1825-26 bubble and crash

- Britain: 624 joint-stock companies by 1824; the crash came in 1825 and bankruptcies nearly doubled in 1826. Sources disagree on how many banks failed (the article says both twelve and seventy) ([Wikipedia, Panic of 1825](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_of_1825)). Treat bank-failure counts as **Uncertain**.
- Greek loans: 1824 loan reported as 800,000 pounds of 5% bonds issued at 59; 1825 loan 2,000,000 pounds issued at 56.5; bonds fell to 10.5 at default in July 1827, down 82% from the 1825 issue price ([Finaeon via search results](https://www.finaeon.com/?p=6732)). Other sources give different sizes and default dates (1826 vs 1827) ([Reinhart and Trebesch appendix](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/ReinhartTrebesch_GreekDebtCrisis_OnlineAppendix.pdf)). **Uncertain.**
- Crisis broke in London in December 1825 ([search summary of Winton, The Panic of 1825](https://www.winton.com/longer-view/the-panic-of-1825)).
- Frankfurt-specific price behaviour (for example Metalliques or Prussian bonds) was **not found**. **Uncertain.** The Rothschild archive paper (Flandreau and Flores) would be the source to read; the fetch failed on a certificate error.

## 1847-48

- Business crisis began in England at the end of 1847 and spread to the continent. After the Feb 1848 Paris revolution there was a run on hard currency. Railway shares, state bonds and bank shares fell; by early summer 1848 many had lost over half their value ([Chastain, Encyclopedia of 1848 Revolutions](https://people.ohio.edu/chastain/dh/econcris.htm)). This is Germany-wide, not Frankfurt-specific.
