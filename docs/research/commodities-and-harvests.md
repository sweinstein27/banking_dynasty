# Commodities, fairs, harvests and the Zollverein

Research note for the game. Commodity-level price data for Frankfurt was **not found** in this pass; most of this file is about timing and direction, not numbers. Everything unsourced is flagged **Uncertain**.

## Frankfurt fairs

- Easter (spring) fair: imperial grant of 25 Apr 1330 (Louis the Bavarian), 14 days, mainly winter goods such as wool and wine. Autumn fair: tied to 15 August, privilege from Frederick II in 1240 ([German Wikipedia, Frankfurter Messe](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Messe)).
- From about 1830 the fairs had only the character of a funfair (Jahrmarktscharakter); a letter of 13 Mar 1835 is captioned as marking the end of a fair (same page). English Wikipedia also says the fair was of little importance in the Free City period ([Free City of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Frankfurt)).
- The decline is blamed on the Revolutionary-era losses, the Continental Blockade, the Zollverein and industrialisation (German Wikipedia above).
- One search summary of a Historisches Museum Frankfurt object text says local trade "flourished again" after Frankfurt regained a fair monopoly. The page was not fetchable (404) and the date is unknown. **Uncertain.**
- No fair turnover, stall counts or goods-by-value for 1815-1850 were found.

## Zollverein and Frankfurt

- Zollverein began 1 Jan 1834. Hesse-Homburg joined 20 Feb 1835, Baden 12 May 1835, Nassau 10 Dec 1835, Frankfurt 2 Jan 1836 ([Wikipedia, Zollverein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zollverein)).
- Frankfurt was the only city outside the union after 1834 while its surroundings were inside; trade fell sharply and Offenbach, Hoechst and Bockenheim grew. In 1828 Frankfurt had joined a Middle German trade association against Prussian plans. In 1832 Britain and Frankfurt signed a free trade and shipping contract ([Free City of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Frankfurt)).
- Accession treaty concluded in Berlin on 2 Jan 1836; Frankfurt received the rights of an independent member, and a contemporary account says transit trade had been passing the city via Mainz, Hoechst and Offenbach (search summary of Frankfurt and DHM sources; **Uncertain** on wording).
- After the accession, Frankfurt's character shifted toward politics and the bourse (same summary, **Uncertain**).
- Wikipedia notes revisionist views that the Zollverein did not greatly change industrial investment; Prussian tariffs hit textiles heavily (Zollverein page). No Frankfurt tariff or revenue numbers were found.
- Population context: city territory 63,151 in 1837 (54,037 in the city area), 91,150 in 1864 ([Free City of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Frankfurt)).

## For the game

- Seasonal rhythm: two fair quarters per year (Easter Q2, Autumn Q3) as trade-event hooks, with fair strength fading from about 1830 (real: funfair character by 1830).
- Harvest shocks as events: 1816-17 (price spike, no hard multiples, so tune), 1846-47 (spring 1847 peak, relief at autumn 1847 harvest). Use the Berlin rye doubling as a loose upper-bound anchor only if labelled as a Prussian figure.
- Customs: a "Frankfurt outside the union" window 1 Jan 1834 to 2 Jan 1836 as a trade drag on the city's merchant clients, then a lift from Q1 1836. Magnitudes are a design choice.
- Goods list for data: grain, wine, wool and cloth, iron, colonial wares; prices and volumes are invented and kept in `data/tuning.json`.
