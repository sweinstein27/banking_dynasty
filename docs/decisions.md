| 2026-11-05 | How do ventures work? | `invest_venture` (one action) locks free cash above the reserve into a venture type from data/ventures.json (minimum 5,000 fl.). The capital earns nothing and cannot be withdrawn until the term ends (counting the quarter of investment, like loans). At the end of its last quarter one roll decides it: failure chance is `failure_risk * m_sentiment` (capped at 1) and loses the whole stake; success pays back the stake times `1 + expected_return`. Ventures count at cost in net worth until they resolve. Ventures not yet due draw no random number. Head of house Acumen will adjust success in Week 5. |
| 2026-11-05 | Where do ventures sit in forced sales? | Last, after bonds and loans, at a 50% discount on the stake (`forced_sale.venture_discount`), selling only the slice needed. The rest still pays out in proportion at maturity. |
| 2026-11-05 | Second golden run added. | "golden run: full economy" (seed 1815, 20 turns): sets a 20,000 reserve, buys bonds rotating through four states, lends to good borrowers, and backs a mill and then a shipping line every fourth turn. The first golden run (lending only) is kept unchanged so earlier balance changes stay comparable. With seed 1815 the full-economy run stays calm all 20 turns, so it does not exercise runs or forced sales; unit tests cover those. |
| 2026-11-05 | **To verify:** the three venture types in data/ventures.json. | "Rhine shipping line" is available from 1815, but Rhine steamship companies date from the mid 1820s (the Prussian-Rhenish steamship company is usually given as 1826 or 1827); before then a shipping venture would be sail or towed barges, which may need a different name. | 2026-11-05 | How do state bonds work? | `buy_bond` (one action) buys face value at the current market price, gated by the state's `min_reputation` and by free cash above the reserve. Coupons are paid quarterly on face value, including the quarter of purchase. Price is `1 - price_sensitivity * risk * (m - 1)` (floor 0.2): par when calm, lower when uneasy or panicking, recovering when calm returns. Holdings are marked to the new mood's price at the end of each turn, with a `revaluation` ledger entry, so the bonds account always equals face times price. Each quarter one default roll is made per state held (`p0 * m * risk`); a default writes off every holding of that state with no recovery, and the state sells no new bonds. States not held are not rolled, so holding nothing draws no extra randomness. No underwriting fee and no maturity for now. No voluntary sale (agreed default). |
| 2026-11-05 | Where do bonds sit in forced sales? | First, before loans, as the most liquid asset: oldest holding first, at the last market price less 10% (`forced_sale.bond_discount`), selling only the slice of face value needed. |
| 2026-11-05 | **To verify:** the five bond issuers in data/states.json. | Prussia, Bavaria, the Austrian Empire, France (Bourbon Restoration) and Naples all existed as states in 1815. Naples became part of the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies in December 1816, so the name should probably change with the date. Coupons (5%, Naples 6%), risk scores and reputation gates are illustrative game numbers, not historical yields. Prussia's gate (55) cannot be reached until reputation can change (events in Week 4, family in Week 5). |
| 2026-11-05 | Should forced sales sell whole loans or only the slice needed? | Only the slice needed. Selling whole loans let a small shortfall cost a large discount (seed 38: a 4,390 fl. shortfall sold a 60,000 fl. loan for 42,000, losing 18,000 fl.). Now the smallest slice of principal whose discounted price covers the shortfall is sold; the rest stays active and keeps earning at its smaller principal. A loan sold down to zero gets status `sold`. Replaces the whole-loan rule in the row below. |
| 2026-11-05 | How do bank runs, forced sales and bankruptcy work? | In a panic depositors withdraw 20% of deposits a quarter (`sentiment.withdrawal_share.panic`). Withdrawals are paid from cash (reserve first). Any shortfall, in any mood, is covered by selling active loans, oldest first, at a 30% discount (`forced_sale.loan_discount`); bonds and ventures join the sale list when they arrive. If everything is sold and depositors still cannot be paid, `house.status` becomes `bankrupt`: End Turn throws and every decision is refused. Withdrawals come after this quarter's interest and repayments, so money arriving that quarter can save the house. Sold loans have status `sold` and write a cash entry for the price and a loans entry for the full principal. |
| 2026-11-05 | What moves market sentiment before the event system exists (Week 4)? | A Markov chain in tuning.json (`sentiment.transitions`): calm to uneasy 8%, uneasy to calm 30% or to panic 10%, panic to uneasy 40%. No direct jump between calm and panic. One roll at the end of each turn sets next quarter's mood, so defaults and withdrawals use the mood the quarter started in. Events will override this in Week 4. |
| 2026-11-05 | Do deposits move outside a panic? | Uneasy depositors withdraw 2% of deposits a quarter (`sentiment.withdrawal_share`). Calm: none. Panic withdrawals (the bank run) arrive in step 3 with forced sales; until then a shortfall is left owed. Inflows driven by reputation are deferred. |

| 2026-11-05 | How do reserves work? | The reserve is a cash floor the player sets with a `set_reserve` action, which uses one of the turn's 3 actions. Lending (and later bonds and ventures) may only spend cash above it. Withdrawals may go below it, and are paid from the reserve first, then from free cash. The floor itself does not shrink after a withdrawal, so new spending stays blocked until income rebuilds cash. A reserve above current cash is allowed. Setting it writes no ledger entry because no money moves. |
| 2026-11-05 | Do the 3 actions per turn apply to every decision type? | Yes. `actions_per_turn` moved from `loans` to the top level of tuning.json, and the state tracks `actions_this_turn`, reset at End Turn. |
| 2026-11-05 | What happens in Week 1 to systems the spec leaves for later? | Sentiment stays calm, the base rate is fixed, deposits do not move or pay interest, and there is no bankruptcy check. Lending is refused if it would push cash below zero. Net worth can go negative after defaults until Week 2 adds bankruptcy. |
| 2026-11-05 | When does a new loan start earning, and in what order does a quarter resolve? | Loans are made at the start of the quarter, so they earn interest that quarter. Order: apply decisions, roll defaults, collect interest from loans that did not default, repay loans at the end of their term, advance the calendar. |
| 2026-11-05 | The spec's ledger_entry has no way to show non-cash changes such as a default. | Added an `account` column (cash, loans, deposits). A loan writes two entries (cash out, loans in), a default writes one loans entry. Summing entries per account reproduces each balance, so any run can be reconciled. Opening balances are written as `opening` entries. |
| 2026-11-05 | Can the player pick the loan term? | No. The term is fixed at 4 quarters (`loans.term_quarters` in tuning.json). |
| 2026-11-05 | What is lost when a loan defaults? | The full principal, and no interest that quarter. A partial recovery rate may come in Week 2. |
| 2026-11-05 | If the player sets the rate, what stops them always picking the highest? | Three borrower tiers (poor q=0.3, fair q=0.6, good q=0.9). Each tier has a maximum rate it will accept, higher for riskier borrowers. An offer above that maximum is refused. Values live in tuning.json. |
| 2026-11-05 | What currency and units does the ledger use? | Frankfurt gulden (fl.), stored as whole integers to avoid rounding drift. Interest is rounded to the nearest gulden. Starting cash 100,000 fl., deposits 50,000 fl., reputation 50. **To verify:** that the gulden was Frankfurt's money of account in 1815, and whether these starting sums are plausible for a mid-sized house. |
