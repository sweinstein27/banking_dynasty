# A3 markets: decisions

Decisions from the A3 session (branch `claude/a3-markets-e93df9`), in the style of docs/decisions.md, for the integrator to move there. Plan approved by Scott on 2026-11-06. Historical sources: docs/research/bourse-1815-1850.md, commodities-and-harvests.md and utilities.md (history-research branch, commit 453152c).

| Date | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-11-06 | Where does the markets code live? | The seam stays `src/sim/systems/markets.ts`; the pieces are in `src/sim/markets/` (types, prices, holdings, stream). Ventures stay in `src/sim/ventures.ts`. Data: `data/markets.json` (commodities, sectors, companies, regions, harvests, shocks, news, reports) and `data/ventures.json`; tuning in the `ventures` and `forced_sale` sections of `data/tuning.json`. |
| 2026-11-06 | How do markets roll dice without shifting every other system's rolls? | Markets and ventures draw from their own stream. `createMarkets` takes one draw from the run's RNG to seed a mulberry32 stream (`src/sim/rng.ts`, unchanged), and its state is kept in `state.markets.rng_state`. The stream resumes exactly from a save, and the same seed gives the same run. Holding nothing on the markets changes no other roll, so only the goldens with ventures moved. |
| 2026-11-06 | When do prices move? | In two End Turn steps. `resolveMarkets` runs where the seam was, after ventures: companies earn, may fail, pay year-end dividends, and goods pay storage. A new `markMarkets` runs after `markBonds`, once next quarter's mood is rolled and its events have fired: the Q3 harvest, shocks from events just fired and from the markets' own news, listings due next quarter, then new prices and revaluation entries. So the price the player trades at already reflects the news they are reading. This adds one line to `src/sim/turn.ts`. |
| 2026-11-06 | How are commodities priced? | Five goods: grain, wine, cloth, iron and colonial wares. Each quarter, log price closes `reversion` of the gap to a target, plus a uniform move of up to plus or minus `volatility`, kept within `bounds` times base times level. Target = base x permanent level x temporary shock x harvest index ^ -elasticity (grain on all regions, wine on the Rhine) x a fair lift in Q2 and Q3 for wine and cloth. Prices and units are invented game numbers: research found no Frankfurt commodity prices. |
| 2026-11-06 | How do holdings in goods work? | Bought and sold at the last price plus 1% commission, within warehouse space (`space` per unit). Each quarter a share spoils (grain 2%, wine and colonial wares 0.5%) and storage is charged per unit held, from cash including the reserve. If cash cannot cover storage, just enough goods are sold at the forced-sale discount; anything still uncovered is written off by the warehouse, so cash never goes below zero. |
| 2026-11-06 | Warehouse space, before A2 merges. | `warehouseSpace(state, data)` in `src/sim/markets/holdings.ts` returns `data.markets.warehouse.default_space` (2,000). Proposed to A2 in the shared notes: A2 exports `warehouseCapacity(state, data)` and the body becomes a call to it. A3 charges per unit of goods held; the building's upkeep stays A2's, so nothing is charged twice. |
| 2026-11-06 | A2's warehouse hook (from A2's shared note). | A2's `warehouseSpace(state, data)` in counting.ts returns lots: 0, or 40 with a warehouse. **At merge:** A3's `warehouseSpace` returns 50 units per lot, so 40 lots equals the current 2,000 units. With no warehouse the house holds no goods, so the grain-trading harness strategies (investor, aggressive) need A2's warehouse first. A3's decision kinds count as deals in A2's capacity pools; suggested to A2 that `company_report` use the reports pool. |
| 2026-11-06 | How are companies priced? | Quarterly earnings = earning capital x yearly yield / 4 x (mood factor x driver x growth + noise x u), u uniform in [-1, 1). The noise is additive, so a bad quarter can lose money. Drivers: shipping and canals follow the harvest, industry the iron price, textiles cloth. Growth runs 1% a year (railways 3%) up to a cap. Fair value = trailing four quarters' dividend per share / (required yield + the mood's premium), or 20% of par for a company paying nothing. Price closes 35% of the gap to fair value x shock, moves by up to the sector's price noise, and stays within 0.3 to 2.5 times fair value and above 1 fl. Dividends are paid at Q4: payout share of the year's earnings. |
| 2026-11-06 | Can a company fail? | Yes. Each quarter the chance is the sector's `failure` x the mood multiplier, at full strength after a trailing year of losses and at 10% of it otherwise (`prices.sound_failure`). A failure writes the house's shares off (`company_failure`). A floated venture whose company fails is called on no more. |
| 2026-11-06 | Report tiers for company books. | Like A1's borrower reports, but in A3's own module for now (`earningsEstimate`): bourse talk is free (plus or minus 40%), an annual report costs 500 fl. (15%, four quarters), a full inquiry costs 2,000 fl. (exact, four quarters). The range is centred on a fixed per-company draw, so it always contains the truth and cannot be re-rolled. **For after merging:** share this code with A1's tiers. |
| 2026-11-06 | Which companies list, and when? | Nine invented companies. **Before 1835:** Danube Privileged Bank (bank, 1820 Q1), Rhenish Fire Assurance (insurance, 1825 Q1), Rhine Steam Packet Company (shipping, 1827 Q2), Main Gas Light Company (utility, 1828 Q3), Main Waterworks Company (utility, 1834 Q4). **From 1835:** Nidda Valley Railway (railway, 1838 Q3), Saar Ironworks Company (industry, 1840 Q1), Continental Gas Society (utility, 1844 Q1), Upper Rhine Railway (railway, 1845 Q2). Floated ventures add their own companies. |
| 2026-11-06 | **To verify:** company dates. | Sourced: the first share traded in Frankfurt was the Austrian National Bank's, in 1820 (the bank); the Taunus railway company was founded 12 Aug 1838, its shares oversubscribed 40 times (the Nidda Valley Railway); Frankfurt's first gasworks dates from 1828 (Knoblauch and Schiele, private) and the ICGA's Frankfurt works from 1844 (Main Gas Light, Continental Gas). **Uncertain:** the fire insurer in 1825 (no source; Aachen-style insurers of the 1820s); Rhine steamships in 1827 (the Cologne steamship company is usually given as 1826 or 1827); the waterworks in 1834 (one secondary source says Frankfurt's water supply was built 1827 to 1834, with no detail of who financed it, so a listed company is a design choice); Saar ironworks shares in 1840 and the Upper Rhine Railway in 1845 (invented; the real Main-Neckar line of 1846 was state-backed). |
| 2026-11-06 | How do harvests work? | Three regions (the Rhine and Hesse, the north and east, the south) are rolled at the end of each Q3: failed, poor, fair, good or a bumper crop (weights 5, 20, 45, 22, 8). A shared weather draw is followed by each region 60% of the time, so bad years come together. The roll always draws the same numbers, so a historic year does not shift the stream. The harvest sets grain's and wine's targets and the earnings of shipping and canals, and news reports it. |
| 2026-11-06 | **To verify:** historic harvests. | Fixed by history: 1816 failed on the Rhine and in the south and poor in the north (Tambora; the coldest summer on record; Wuerttemberg hit hardest); 1817 fair; 1845 poor everywhere; 1846 failed in the north and east (Prussia 30 to 50% below earlier years, a figure seen only via a search summary) and poor elsewhere; 1847 a bumper crop (sourced: it ended the crisis). The 1817 "fair" and the regional split are design choices. |
| 2026-11-06 | Harvests and borrowers. | `tradeFactor(state, trade, data)` (exported from the seam) is a multiplier on a borrower's default chance: grain dealers (elasticity 1.5), wine importers (Rhine, 1) and river freight (1) after a bad harvest, capped at 2. **Not wired:** the loan default roll is A1's. Suggested one line: multiply the default chance by `tradeFactor(state, borrower.trade, data)`. Noted for A1 in the shared notes. |
| 2026-11-06 | What moves prices on the news? | `event_shocks` maps existing events (data/events.json, unchanged) to shocks. London 1825: shares 0.9, then 0.8 when the banks fail. Panic of 1826: 0.9. July Revolution 1830: 0.88. The Zollverein in 1834 lowers cloth and colonial wares (Frankfurt was left outside), and Frankfurt joining in 1836 lifts them. Commercial crisis 1847: shares 0.8. February 1848: 0.75. March 1848: 0.85. With the mood, shares lose about half from early 1847 to mid 1848, matching the sourced "over half". The markets' own news: London speculation 1825 Q2, railway shares oversubscribed 1838 Q3, the Rhine crisis 1840 Q3, railway shares soar 1844 Q2. Temporary shocks fade 25% a quarter; levels are permanent. |
| 2026-11-06 | **To verify:** the markets' own news dates. | The Rhine crisis of 1840 (summer to autumn 1840) is from general knowledge, not the research notes. The 1844 railway boom rests on a boerse.de summary of a 1842 to 1848 railway share boom (page not fetched) and the Duesseldorf-Elberfeld rise of late 1842 to April 1843. The London speculation of 1825 is sourced. |
| 2026-11-06 | The Frankfurt fairs. | Wine and cloth get a lift in Q2 (Easter fair) and Q3 (autumn fair), fading from full strength in 1830 to 30% by 1836, since by about 1830 the fairs were little more than funfairs (sourced). The size of the lift is a design choice. |
| 2026-11-06 | How do ventures work now? | `invest_venture` commits an amount (at least 5,000 fl.) and pays the first call; only that first call needs free cash. The rest is called in equal instalments: each quarter for short and medium ventures, each year for great works. Each call after the first may overrun, raising the commitment by 8 to 10% and adding a call. While building there is a failure roll each quarter: the type's whole-build risk spread evenly, scaled by mood, Acumen and traits. A failure loses the stake less a 10% salvage. A call that finds too little cash forfeits 25% of the stake, and no more calls come. Ventures count at cost in net worth until they list. |
| 2026-11-06 | Venture lengths. | Short (1 to 2 years: brewery 4 quarters, tobacco manufactory 6) pay out once when built and never list. Medium (3 to 8 years counting the proving time: textile mill, Rhine shipping line, ironworks from 1820, gasworks from 1828) run once built and pay year-end dividends. Great works (15 to 25 years: railway 15 years from 1836, canal 16 years from 1836, deep coal mine 15 years from 1838, trunk railway 22 years from 1842) open in sections: each earns as it opens, so a great work pays long before it is finished. |
| 2026-11-06 | When does a venture list? | Once it has run for its `prove_quarters` and its last four quarters earned at least 4% of the stake. The stake becomes shares at 250 fl. par; the whole company is the stake divided by the house's `stake_share` (20% for a railway); the price opens at 90% of fair value. Later calls on a listed great work buy new shares at par. A listed company's earning capital follows its open sections until the build is done. |
| 2026-11-06 | Selling a stake before listing. | `sell_venture` (uses capacity) sells an unlisted stake privately at a 40% discount (`ventures.unlisted_discount`), with no more calls. Forced sales of unlisted stakes take the larger of that and `forced_sale.venture_discount` (30%), so a bank run never sells a stake more cheaply than choice would. A forced sale scales the calls still to come by the share of the stake kept. |
| 2026-11-06 | Market holdings in a bank run. | `raiseCash` sells shares (5% off the last price) and then goods (15% off) after bonds and before loans, only as much as needed (`raiseFromMarkets`). That is one line in `src/sim/liquidity.ts`, plus two keys in `forced_sale`. |
| 2026-11-06 | Ledger accounts and categories. | New accounts `shares` and `commodities` (`MarketsAccount`). Their balances equal the holdings at their last prices (`sharesBook`, `commoditiesBook`) and go into net worth through `marketsBook`; the save check verifies both. New categories: share purchase and sale, dividend, commodity purchase and sale, storage, spoilage, market revaluation, venture call, venture dividend, venture listing, venture sale, venture forfeit, company failure, company report. Commissions and storage leave as cash only. |
| 2026-11-06 | Capacity. | Every market decision checks and uses capacity only through `checkCapacity` and `useCapacity`, with its own kind: `buy_shares`, `sell_shares`, `buy_commodity`, `sell_commodity`, `company_report`, `sell_venture`. Calls, dividends and storage use none. |
| 2026-11-06 | Venture news. | `news.ts` now reads a venture's paid-out or failed stake from that quarter's ventures-account entries, not from the start of the quarter, so a call paid in the same quarter counts. The markets write their own news (kind `"markets"`, added to `NewsKind`): harvests, listings, failures, overruns, forfeits, floats and the markets' news items. |
| 2026-11-06 | Harness. | New strategy `investor`: a 20% reserve, then the bourse and grain. It sells shares above 1.3 times fair value and goods whose price has caught up, buys grain when the target is 12% above the price, and buys the best-yielding shares that pay at least their sector's required yield, keeping each company under a third of its market book. With what is left it lends a little to the safest borrower. Market makers for the others (late, so they use leftover actions): cautious buys utilities with spare cash; aggressive buys railway and industry shares and speculates in grain; bond-heavy buys banks, insurance and utilities; venture-heavy also commits to a railway and a gasworks every two years. Venture commitments are sized on free cash less calls still owed (`shareOfUncommitted`), a light change to `strategies.ts`. |
| 2026-11-06 | First tuning. | 100-seed runs showed venture-heavy at a 1.46M median: short ventures paid 25 to 40% on capital called gradually, about double that on capital actually tied up. Short returns fell to 12% (brewery) and 20% (tobacco). Medium and great venture yields fell about 2 points (railway 7%, mill 9%). Share growth fell to 1% (railways 3%). Required yields fell by about 1.5 points toward bond yields (utilities 3.8%, railways 4%, industry 5%). Sound companies can now fail too, rarely. The investor's median went from 409k to 173k with a real spread. |
| 2026-11-06 | Harness, 1,000 seeds. | See "Harness results" below: T2 and T6 fail on A3 (all pass on main), mainly because venture-heavy and aggressive now rarely go bankrupt. Left for balancing after merging. |

Targets on A3: T1, T3, T4, T5 and T7 pass. **T2 fails** (aggressive dominates bond-heavy on median, p90 and bankruptcy) and **T6 fails** (aggressive bankrupt in 27.0% of runs, against 30 to 60%). On main all seven pass. Not tuned further: balance is measured after merging (docs/parallel.md rule 4), and the main levers there are outside A3 (lending, bonds, the counting house's capacity).

What changed and why, from flows.csv (average per run):

- **Venture-heavy** went from the weakest to the strongest strategy. Its capital is now called in instalments and a floated stake turns into shares it can sell cheaply in a run, so it almost never goes bankrupt (44.1% to 1.4%). Per run: short ventures +164k fl. paid out over stake against 135k fl. lost to failures; dividends on floated ventures 111k fl.; share revaluation +32k fl.; company failures -30k fl.
- **Investor:** dividends 154k fl., revaluation +31k fl., company failures -41k fl.; grain roughly breaks even after storage (about -4k fl.). Its p10 (70k fl.) is far above every other strategy's: shares carry too little ruin risk compared with bonds and loans. **For after merging:** more equity tail risk (deeper panic falls, dividend cuts) or a lower investor reserve to test.
- **Aggressive** is safer than on main (33.0% to 27.0% bankrupt): its shipping venture now draws cash over two years instead of all at once, and its railway shares are a liquid buffer.
- **Cautious** barely uses the markets: its utilities purchase needs 30,000 fl. of free cash and an action left over, and it seldom has both (dividends of about 400 fl. a run).
- Floating opens on average slightly below the stake (listing gain about -3k fl. a run for venture-heavy), because a great work floats on its first section's earnings, in whatever mood the quarter has.

Tuning between the first and second 1,000-seed runs: medium and great venture yields set so a floated venture opens about 15% above cost (yield x payout / required yield x 0.9 near 1.15: mill 8%, shipping 7.7%, ironworks 8%, gasworks 6%, railway 6.4%, canal 6.4%, mine 8%, trunk railway 6.4%), and aggressive's railway and industry purchases cut from 30% to 15% of free cash. The first run had venture-heavy at a 208,566 fl. median with 1.6% bankrupt, and aggressive at 25.8% bankrupt (T5 also failed at 11.9%).

Other checks: `npm run typecheck` and `npm test` pass (484 tests, 5 skipped: the goldens and one news test that depend on the old venture rules). A production build of the UI succeeds and the Venture dialog compiles unchanged; it sends the amount as a commitment, and LedgerBook's "pays X in N qtr" line is now only right for short ventures. Not checked in the browser: port 5173 was in use by another session.

## Files touched outside A3's ownership

Light changes, each needed for the system to work:

- `src/sim/turn.ts`: one line calling `markMarkets` after `markBonds`, and the End Turn comment.
- `src/sim/liquidity.ts`: bank runs sell shares and goods after bonds (`raiseFromMarkets`); unlisted stakes sell at no less than the private-sale discount; a stake sold to nothing has no more calls.
- `src/sim/news.ts`: the paid-out or failed venture stake comes from the quarter's ventures-account entries.
- `src/sim/save.ts`: `shares` and `commodities` in the books check; a check that `state.markets` is complete.
- `src/sim/types.ts`: the `Venture` type (allowed) and `"markets"` in `NewsKind`.
- `src/sim/tuning.ts`: `VentureType` (tiers, calls, overruns, sections, sector, listing), the `ventures` section (allowed) and two keys in `forced_sale` (`share_discount`, `commodity_discount`).
- `data/tuning.json`: the `ventures` section (allowed) and the same two `forced_sale` keys.
- `src/data.ts`: `ventures` cast to `VentureType[]` (the JSON's `tier` string).
- `src/harness/strategies.ts`: the `investor` strategy in the list (allowed); aggressive's and venture-heavy's commitments sized on free cash less calls still owed.
- `scripts/play.ts`: the `[m] Markets` menu, the markets line in the status, and holdings; the menu itself is in the new `scripts/markets-menu.ts`.
- Tests: `tests/family.test.ts` and `tests/news.test.ts` (venture assertions rewritten for the new rules; one news test skipped), `tests/golden.test.ts` (three skips, each with an A3 comment).

Not touched: `src/ui/`. The Venture dialog and LedgerBook compile unchanged.
