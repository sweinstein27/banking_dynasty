# A1, credit: decisions

In the style of docs/decisions.md. The integrator moves these rows there after merging.

| Date | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-11-06 | What replaces the quality estimate? | Borrowers' books. Each borrower keeps books built from its hidden soundness (`quality`) plus five fixed quirks: yearly turnover, true margin, book margin (the true margin plus up to `dressing x (1 - quality)`, so weak houses flatter their books), debts owed to others, collateral, the year it began trading, and late payments to other lenders over the last 8 quarters. The formulas and numbers are in data/credit.json (`books`). No verdict or decimal is shown anywhere, and defaults still roll on the hidden quality. |
| 2026-11-06 | How do the books change? | Each quarter after loans are serviced (`resolveCredit`), turnover moves by up to +/- 5% at random, plus +1% calm, -2% uneasy, -6% panic, minus `drift x (trade_risk - 1)`. Margins shift with the mood (0, -1%, -3%), debts and collateral follow turnover, and a late payment is recorded with chance `0.15 x m_sentiment x (1 - quality)`. |
| 2026-11-06 | Which RNG moves the books? | Credit's own stream, seeded by one roll of the main RNG at new game and kept in `state.credit.rng_state` (mulberry32 from rng.ts, unchanged). So End Turn draws no extra main rolls for the books and other systems' roll-counting tests are unaffected. Requests draw six main rolls each (was two): pick, kind, amount, term, whether the talk is true, a random band. Creating borrowers draws nine each (was three). |
| 2026-11-06 | What information can the player buy? | Three tiers. **Bourse talk** (free, uses no capacity): one line per request naming a band (sound, steady, stretched, doubtful); it is true with chance `0.60 + 0.03 x Prudence`, at most 0.90, otherwise a band at random. **Correspondent's report** (300 fl., capacity kind `report`): turnover, book profit, debts, late payments. **Full inquiry** (1,200 fl., capacity kind `inquire`): all of that plus collateral, the true profit and a partner's private remark (a band from quality +/- 0.05). Always visible: name, trade, years in trade, record with the house, kind, term, amount and the highest rate. |
| 2026-11-06 | Are reports permanent? | No. A report or inquiry is a snapshot of the books in the quarter it was bought (`Borrower.file`). It can be bought again once it is 4 quarters old (`info.stale_quarters`). An inquiry can follow a report at once. Files can be bought on any borrower asking for a loan or owing the house, so the player can check a loan before selling it. |
| 2026-11-06 | What does Prudence do now? | It makes bourse talk more often true. The old range narrowing is gone. The Prudent and Reckless traits still move Prudence by 2. |
| 2026-11-06 | What kinds of loan are there? | In tuning.json `loans.kinds`. **Bill of exchange**: 45% of requests, 1 to 2 quarters, 1,000 to 6,000 fl., rate -1%. **Commercial credit**: 40%, 4 to 12 quarters, 5,000 to 20,000 fl., +0.25% a year beyond the first. **Mortgage / long credit**: 15%, 20 to 40 quarters, 15,000 to 60,000 fl. but never above 75% of the borrower's collateral (else the request becomes commercial credit), +1%, default chance x 0.6, principal repaid in equal parts each quarter, and on default 60% of the principal is recovered, scaled down when collateral covers less than the principal. The rate rule is otherwise unchanged: `base + spread x (1 - market_quality)` plus the premium, to the nearest 0.5%, never below 0.5%. Interest is paid quarterly on the principal still owed. |
| 2026-11-06 | How many requests and borrowers? | Four requests a quarter (was three), so bills come often. Still one active loan per borrower, so the borrower list grew from 12 to 20 invented houses (data/borrowers.json); long loans would otherwise empty the pool. |
| 2026-11-06 | How does selling a loan work? | New decision `sell_loan` (capacity kind `sell_loan`): another house buys the whole loan for `principal x (1 - discount)`, discount `0.03 + 0.15 x (1 - market_quality) + 0.01 x years left + mood` (0 calm, 0.05 uneasy, 0.15 panic), at most 0.6 (data/credit.json `sale`). The buyer prices on the market's view, not the truth, so a loan the market rates too highly is worth selling. Ledger: `loan_sale` on cash (price) and loans (principal). Forced sales in a run are unchanged (flat 30%, slices). |
| 2026-11-06 | How is a mortgage default reported? | News says what the collateral brought back when it brought anything (one line changed in src/sim/news.ts), and the ledger has a `recovery` cash entry. Event defaults (`loan_default`) recover the same way, since they go through `recordDefault`. |
| 2026-11-06 | How do the harness strategies read the books? | `readBooks` in src/harness/systems/credit.ts inverts the books' formulas as a careful clerk would (no hidden quality, no quirks): bourse talk alone moves a 0.6 prior halfway to the band's guess; a report averages the quality implied by book margin (less an allowance for dressing) and by debts, minus 0.05 per late payment; an inquiry averages true margin, debts, collateral and twice the remark. Tested: an inquiry reads quality closer than a report. **Cautious** (and cautious without family) picks the most valuable request without a fresh file that the bourse calls sound or steady, is not a bill and asks 8,000 fl. or more; it buys a full inquiry if that request is a mortgage, else a report; lends only when its reading is 0.65 or more; and when worried, reports on its largest loan and sells it if the reading is below 0.55. **Bond-heavy** buys nothing, as before A1, and lends a little to the best reading on bourse talk. **Aggressive** and **venture-heavy** buy nothing and go on rate alone. |
| 2026-11-06 | Why does cautious not buy a file on every request? | The first version did (an inquiry on anything of 10,000 fl. or more, a report otherwise). Over 40 seeds it spent 95,000 fl. a run on information (82,080 on inquiries, 13,080 on reports) against 41,491 fl. of interest, bought 112 files for 62 loans, and went bankrupt in 16 of 40 seeds. A 1,200 fl. inquiry on a 10,000 fl. two-year loan costs most of its interest, and a file on a bill never pays. With the filter above: 1 of 40 bankrupt, 94 loans a run. Bond-heavy buying reports cost it one of three actions and about 34,000 fl. a run (16 of 40 bankrupt); without them, 8 of 40. |
| 2026-11-06 | Golden runs and saves. | The four golden snapshot tests (three "matches the recorded result" and the panic run's coverage check) are skipped with an A1 comment; the integrator re-records them and re-picks `PANIC_SEED`. `SAVE_VERSION` is not bumped, though `Borrower`, `Loan` and `LoanRequest` changed shape. |
| 2026-11-06 | Harness result (1,000 seeds). | Four of seven targets fail, all from aggressive lending getting stronger; see the table below. Not retuned before merging. |
| 2026-11-06 | **To verify:** bill terms. | Bills of exchange in the period commonly ran at "usance" of one to three months, sometimes longer for distant places; 1 to 2 quarters may be long for Frankfurt inland bills. Not checked against a source. |
| 2026-11-06 | **To verify:** a premium for length. | As agreed, longer loans carry a higher rate. Historically, mortgage credit in the German states (for example the Prussian Landschaften and their Pfandbriefe) was often cheaper than unsecured merchant credit, around 4 to 5%, because it was secured on land. The game's premium is a design choice, not history. |
| 2026-11-06 | **To verify:** rate levels and usury limits. | Several German states capped interest rates in this period (commonly cited at 5 to 6% for ordinary loans), with exceptions for merchants and bills. Whether Frankfurt had such a cap, and whether rates up to about 20% for a weak borrower are plausible, is unchecked. Game rates are illustrative. |
| 2026-11-06 | **To verify:** amortizing mortgages. | Repayment in equal parts follows the amortizing loans of the Landschaften; whether a Frankfurt private bank would lend this way to a merchant is unchecked. |
| 2026-11-06 | **To verify:** selling loans to other houses. | Rediscounting bills with another house was common; selling a long commercial loan or a mortgage outright to another private bank is less certain for 1815 to 1850. |
| 2026-11-06 | Names. | The eight new borrowers (Holzapfel, Kirchner and Lang, Seidel, Moll, Pfeiffer und Toechter, Ebert, Lorenz, Kuehn and Hesse) are invented, like the first twelve. The trades are plausible for Frankfurt but unchecked. |

Before A1 (main at 450eda8) and after (this branch), seeds 1 to 1000. Net worth in 1850; a strong finish is 150,000 fl. or more.

Targets after: T1 pass, T2 **fail** (aggressive dominates bond_heavy and venture_heavy), T3 pass, T4 pass (3.0%), T5 **fail** (aggressive 9.5% bankrupt in 1825 to 1829, needs 12%), T6 **fail** (aggressive 20.3% bankrupt, needs 30 to 60%), T7 **fail** (cautious 44,304 against 52,390 without family, needs 90%). Before A1 all seven passed.

Why: the failures all come from aggressive lending getting much stronger. Over 40 seeds it now earns about 406,000 fl. of loan interest a run against 141,000 fl. of defaults (28,000 recovered). Four requests a quarter, terms up to ten years and amounts up to 60,000 fl. let it keep most of its capital lent at the highest rates, and the rate rule (`base + spread x (1 - market_quality)`, up to about 20%) pays far more than the default chance costs (about 3% a year for a weak borrower in calm, and less for mortgages, which also recover 60%). Fewer forced sales mean fewer bankruptcies. Cautious pays about 53,000 fl. a run for files and lends less, so its median falls. T7 fails because cautious with family spends more on files than before. Per docs/parallel.md, balance is measured after merging (and A2's capacity changes every strategy), so I have not retuned. The levers are all in credit's data: the rate spread or kind premiums, mortgage default_factor and recovery, requests_per_quarter, and info costs.
